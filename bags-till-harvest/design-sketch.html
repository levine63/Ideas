Design notes · prototype, not evaluated

Bags Till Harvest

A prototype phone exercise, adapted from the Augenblick et al. field experiment, that helps a Zambian farm family list the year's costs in bags of maize and see whether its store lasts until harvest. By David Levine, September 2026. English only; not tested with farmers; not affiliated with or reviewed by the study's authors.

The live app runs on the left. The notes on the right follow whichever screen it shows: what the study's exercise did, what this version does instead, and what to test with farmers. The buttons above the notes jump the app to a screen with the example family loaded.

What the study did

Augenblick, Jack, Kaur, Masiye and Swanson, Retrieval Failures and Consumption Smoothing (NBER Working Paper 35430, 2026). In Zambia, where most farm households live on one maize harvest a year, a trained surveyor prompted farmers to recall future expenses one category at a time, without advice or outside information. Farmers placed pins standing for bags of grain into category boxes. Prompted recall raised remembered expenses by 36–60%. Treated households held 15% more savings six weeks later and entered the hungry season with about one more month of savings. The 2023 version reported 42% more remembered expenses and measured the savings difference two months later.

These results come from the facilitated exercise. This app has not been evaluated, and its prompts have not yet been checked against the study's surveyor script.

Kept from the study

  • Recall, with light prompts. The family chooses every amount. The exceptions are the three gentle questions (food, school, keep-safe), a default bag price, and short tips such as "slow down early". The food question adds the most outside information and can be switched off.
  • One category at a time, with picture cues. School, Home, Farm, People, Surprises.
  • Bags as the unit, like the pins. Cash costs count as bags that must be sold.

Changed, and why

  • The board is a calendar. Each cost lands on its month, or is spread over the year. Running out is a timing problem, so timing is what the family sees.
  • Guess first, then count. The gap between the family's guess and the count is their own discovery.
  • Play the year. Stacks of sacks empty month by month. Months not reached yet stay blank, so the result is a surprise.
  • Khoswe the rat. Costs nobody can date (clinic, funeral, a broken bike) become keep-safe bags.
  • No celebration for a plan that only works after cuts. Cuts are measured against the family's first complete list.
  • Three check months, not a spreadsheet. August, November and February, with a bag count for each.

The app cannot tell whose phone it is: presenters must use the presenter link. WhatsApp and SMS go from the presenter's account or SIM and stay in its sent messages. Scanning the QR code is more private; the code is drawn without internet, but opening it needs signal on the farmer's phone. Numbers in the SMS example are illustrative. Erase the plan before the next family. (The app says "moon" where these notes say "month".)

Risks to design around

  • Planning can't make grain. A real shortfall is shown as "short by N bags", with a pointer to help; the app must not shame the family.
  • The model tracks maize only. It leaves out other food, cash income and buying grain, so a month with no maize is "a moon with no maize", not proof of hunger.
  • Outside information. The food benchmark (150 kg per person per year) is an unvalidated assumption. The example family's amounts are invented.
  • Privacy. Amounts on a screen can be seen by neighbours; shared phones keep sent messages.
  • Humour and pictures travel badly. The rat, the maize guide and the emoji need local testing.

Suggested first test

About twenty households, one to one, comparing the facilitated pin exercise with this app used beside a facilitator. Measure costs remembered, time taken, whether each screen is understood (ask people to explain it back), privacy, and whether families can state a check-month number two weeks later. Then check grain in store at the first check month.
