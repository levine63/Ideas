Design notes · prototype, not evaluated

Bags Till Harvest

A prototype phone exercise, adapted from the Augenblick et al. field experiment, that helps a Zambian farm family list the year's costs in bags of maize and see whether its store lasts until harvest. By David Levine, September 2026. English only; not tested with farmers; not affiliated with or reviewed by the study's authors.

The live app runs on the left. The notes on the right follow whichever screen it shows: what the study's exercise did, what this version does instead, and what to test with farmers. The buttons above the notes jump the app to a screen with the example family loaded.

What the study did

Augenblick, Jack, Kaur, Masiye and Swanson, Retrieval Failures and Consumption Smoothing (NBER Working Paper 35430, 2026). In Zambia, where most farm households live on one maize harvest a year, a trained surveyor prompted farmers to recall future expenses one category at a time, without advice or outside information, using a board with photo boxes for each category. Farmers placed pins standing for bags of grain. Prompted recall raised remembered expenses by 36–60%. Treated households held 15% more savings six weeks later and entered the hungry season with about one more month of savings. The 2023 version reported 42% more remembered expenses and measured the savings difference two months later.

These results come from the facilitated exercise. This app has not been evaluated. The "In the study" notes for each screen are our reading of the paper; several details (timing after harvest, bags on hand, the food sheet, bag labels) came from a design review and have not been checked against the paper or the surveyor script.

This version (second pass)

  • One question per screen, read aloud. The question is the headline, with one control under it. Pictures are OpenMoji mock-ups until local photos exist.
  • Study order. A guess in bags first, then six categories of costs other than food, then food last.
  • The guess and the recall use the same unit. Play the year puts the first guess beside the remembered bags, the gap the study measured.
  • Start where the farmer is: just harvested, or today with the bags on hand.
  • Take-home in steps: say one number back, mark bags with labels, then reminders on your own phone, or SMS, WhatsApp or a QR code from a borrowed phone, followed by erase.
  • Kept from the first pass: Khoswe the rat guarding kept-safe bags; the food check against a benchmark; no celebration and no reminders for a plan that only lasts after cuts.
  • Outside information behind one switch (?outside=off): the example family, the empty-category hint and the food benchmark.

The app cannot tell whose phone it is, so presenters should use the presenter link. SMS and WhatsApp go from the presenter's SIM or account and stay in its sent messages. The QR code is drawn without internet; opening it needs signal on the farmer's phone.

Risks to design around

  • Planning can't make grain. A shortfall is shown plainly, with "Try a change"; the app must not shame the family.
  • The model tracks maize only. It leaves out other food, cash income and buying grain.
  • Outside information. The food benchmark (150 kg per person per year) is an unvalidated assumption. The example family's amounts are invented.
  • Privacy on shared phones and in groups.
  • Pictures and humour travel badly. OpenMoji placeholders and the rat need local testing.

Suggested first test

About twenty households, one to one, comparing the facilitated pin board with this app beside a facilitator. Measure costs remembered, the gap between guess and recall, time taken, whether each screen is understood, privacy, and whether families can say their first check-month number two weeks later.
